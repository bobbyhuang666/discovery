# Opportunity Track

## Purpose

Determine whether a problem and opportunity deserve investment before committing to a full build.

## Entry signals

- The user has an idea but is unsure whether people need it.
- The current question is product viability, customer demand, or investment priority.
- The proposed solution arrived before credible problem evidence.

## Evidence dimensions

Evaluate separately:

- problem reality;
- affected segment and decision authority;
- frequency or triggering conditions;
- impact per occurrence;
- current alternatives and switching constraints;
- willingness to change or commit resources;
- solution fit;
- feasibility and delivery risk;
- commercial viability only when commercialization is intended.

Low frequency does not invalidate an opportunity when impact is high. Verbal willingness to pay is weak evidence; prefer current spending, time cost, signed pilots, deposits, data access, or real behavior.

## Discovery sequence

1. Describe the last real occurrence or establish why the event has not occurred yet.
2. Identify who experiences, pays for, approves, and benefits from change.
3. Quantify current cost, risk, delay, or lost outcome.
4. Map existing alternatives, workarounds, and reasons for not switching.
5. Separate the underlying job/problem from the proposed solution.
6. Identify the riskiest assumption whose failure would invalidate investment.
7. Design the cheapest decisive experiment.
8. Define success, ambiguity, and kill criteria before running the experiment.

## Gate behavior

Weak evidence does not trigger more feature design. It triggers an experiment, prototype, observation, or research task. A user may explicitly choose an exploratory prototype, but it must be labeled as an experiment rather than a validated product commitment.

## Handoff

Produce `templates/opportunity.yaml`. Do not produce a full implementation roadmap unless evidence justifies the next investment stage.
